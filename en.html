Words for Feelings
A memory game for parents and children · Ages 3–12

Finding words
for our feelings.

Play together and find words for feelings. Explore emotions through the cards and make room for conversation, at your child’s pace.

30 feelings · Illustrations and questions by age · Hebrew or English

How to play

1. Find a pair

Turn over two cards each turn and look for matching illustrations.

2. Name the feeling

Read the feeling’s name together. Parents learn alongside their child.

3. Talk, if you like

Choose a short question or keep playing. There is no right answer and no need to share.

After the game

When something does not work out, try a word from the game:

“You tried and it did not work. Could you be feeling frustrated?”

Before meeting someone new, explore the feeling together:

“We are about to meet new children. Are you excited, or perhaps a little worried?”

When someone is missed, give the feeling a name:

“You wish you could be with them now. Could that be a feeling of missing someone?”

After an effort, notice the good feelings too:

“You worked hard and did it. Do you feel proud of yourself?”

When there is a lot of noise or too much happening, ask gently:

“There is a lot happening. Could you feel overwhelmed and need a quiet moment?”

When something we looked forward to is cancelled, start a conversation:

“You looked forward to it and it did not happen. Could you be feeling disappointed?”

Listen and let your child choose the word that fits. “I don’t know” is an answer too.

Prefer to play at the table?

Print the cards and enjoy playing together at the table.

Frequently asked questions

What if my child does not want to talk?

You can simply play. There is no need to persuade them or ask for an explanation. A parent can share a small example or move on to the next turn. Learning the feeling’s name is a beginning too.

Do we have to ask a question after every pair?

Not at all. The questions are invitations. Skip them or choose just one during the game. Enjoying your time together matters more than getting through every question.

Can we feel several feelings at once?

Yes. We can feel happy and nervous, or angry and sad, at the same time. Choose several cards and offer words without deciding for your child what they feel.

What if my child chooses a feeling I did not expect?

Listen with curiosity. You might ask, “What about this word fits for you?” or gently offer another word. There is no need to correct their answer. “I don’t know” is an answer you can accept too.

What if my child does not know the feeling?

Explain it in simple words and give an everyday example. There is no need to remember everything — familiarity grows over time.

Can parents answer the questions too?

Absolutely. A brief example that suits your child’s age can help them understand the feeling and feel comfortable sharing their own experience.

What if my child interprets the illustration differently?

You can ask, “What in the picture made you think that?” The same illustration can suggest different feelings, creating an opportunity to talk.

Does the game replace emotional therapy?

The game helps families explore feelings and talk together. It does not replace therapy or professional guidance.

Emotion cards

Printing tips

Use thick paper (250–300 gsm), print at 100% scale and turn off “Fit to page”. You can laminate the cards after cutting.
